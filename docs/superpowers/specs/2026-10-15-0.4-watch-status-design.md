# 0.4-watch-status: design

## Goal

Kevin knows whether each KevBox Live customer can actually watch. Today the TVs report only
failures, so a single failure (Nohad's channel-list 513 on 2026-10-15) leaves him guessing whether
the customer recovered or gave up. After this phase a TV also reports when a channel plays, and
kevbox.dev flags any customer whose failure got no good play within 15 minutes. It pings him on
Telegram with advice: turn on the proxy, check the line on the IPTV panel, or "provider problem".

Decisions Kevin made on 2026-10-15 while this was designed:

- The warning goes to kevbox.dev and to Telegram.
- "Can watch" means a live channel played for 30 seconds without stopping.
- A TV becomes "can't watch" 15 minutes after a failure with no good play since.
- Advice depends on the failure, not "try the proxy" every time.
- The server decides (approach A). The TV only reports. A TV that is switched off can't report,
  and a customer who gives up and turns the TV off is the case that matters most.

## Scope

- **OwnTV app (`kevbox/` layer, branch base `kevbox-live`)**: a play watcher that notices 30
  seconds of continuous live playback in the Live TV preview box or full screen, and sends a
  "played OK" note through a new Supabase function. Release 5.1.0.15.
- **kevbox (branch base `main`)**: migration `0016_live_watch.sql` (two columns on `live_device`,
  the note function, an incident table); a 5-minute check route plus systemd timer; Telegram
  sending; a status line in the customer window's KevBox Live box; a "Can't watch now" list on
  the Live errors page.
- **Not in scope**: turning the proxy on automatically, a "channel list loaded" note, quiet hours,
  showing anything on the TV itself, KevBox TV (Kevin's rule: Live and TV data never mix).

## How it works

### The TV

A watcher listens to the Live TV preview engine and the full-screen player. When a live channel
has played for 30 seconds without an error, a stop or a channel change, the TV sends a note:
device id, app version, the time on the TV clock, and whether the proxy was on.

It sends at most one note an hour per TV. The exception is the first good play after any failure
the error reporter recorded in this app process: that note goes out at once, so a recovery reaches
kevbox.dev within a minute. If the call fails (no internet, kevbox.dev down) the TV keeps the
latest note and tries again on the next tick, like the usage check-in. Only the newest note
matters, so it keeps one, not a queue.

The note carries no channel name. "Can they watch" doesn't need it.

### The database

`live_device` gets three columns: `last_play_at` (TV clock time of the play), `last_play_received_at`
(server time) and `last_play_proxy` (boolean). The function `record_live_play(p_device_id,
p_app_version, p_played_at, p_proxy)` updates the caller's own row, creating it if the TV has not
checked in yet. Writes go through the function only, like the existing ones (RLS read-own,
`security definer`, grants to `kevbox_admin`).

A new table `live_watch_incident` keeps one row per TV problem: user id, device id, `started_at`
(server time of the failure that opened it), the failure code and http status, the advice, the
proxy flag, `alerted_at`, `cleared_at`, `cleared_proxy`. The check uses it so each problem pings
once.

### Ordering: which came last, the failure or the play?

Both the failure row's `last_at` and the note's `played_at` come from the same TV's clock. A
wrong TV clock is wrong for both, so comparing the two for one TV is safe. The 15-minute wait uses
server time (`received_at` of the failure), because TV clocks on these boxes are often off by
hours.

### When a TV counts as "can't watch"

Every 5 minutes the check looks at each TV on app version 5.1.0.15 or newer (numeric compare of
the version string). Older TVs never send play notes, and flagging them would ping Kevin about
every old failure.

A TV is "can't watch" when all of these hold:

1. The server received a counting failure from it (list below).
2. No play note has a `played_at` later than that failure's `last_at`.
3. At least 15 minutes have passed since the failure's `received_at`.
4. The failure is less than 24 hours old, so a TV left off for a week doesn't page Kevin when
   0.4 first ships.

Counting failures: every `playlist_*` code, every playback code, every `http_*` code, `net_dns`,
`net_refused`, `net_timeout`, `net_ssl`, every `account_*` code, `stream_lost`, `other`.

Not counting: `guide_*` (channels play without the guide), `net_offline` (the customer's own
internet; a proxy can't fix it) and `behind_live_window` (the player recovers by itself).

One playback failure is not enough. A customer who watched all evening, lost one channel at the
end and turned the TV off would otherwise be flagged. So:

- A **channel-list failure** (`playlist_*`) or an **account failure** (`account_*` code, or
  `account_status` expired/banned/disabled/wrong_login) counts on its own: it blocks every
  channel.
- Any other counting failure needs company: two or more failure reports from the same TV with no
  good play between them. A row's `repeat_count` counts, so one row seen twice is two reports.
  The 15 minutes run from the `received_at` of the second report.

A customer is flagged when any of their TVs is.

Cancelled customers, and customers whose subscription has run out, never ping. kevbox.dev
decides this the way the proxy switch does: the workbook's cancelled flag and
`trex_expiry_date`, where the whole expiry day still counts and no date means expired. Their
customer window shows grey "Subscription ended <date>" (or "Cancelled") instead of red.

No ignore list: Kevin's own and test accounts ping like anyone, so he can test it on release day.
There is no button to dismiss a problem by hand; only a good play clears it, and 7 days greys it.

A problem whose TV never sends another play note stays red, with no reminder pings. After 7 days
with no play it turns grey on kevbox.dev: "No plays for 7 days; problem from <date> never
cleared". It never pings again.

### Advice

Taken from the newest counting failure of the open problem, first match wins:

1. `account_expired`, `account_banned`, `account_disabled`, `account_wrong_login`, or
   `account_status` with one of those values: **"Check the line on the IPTV panel (expired /
   banned / disabled / wrong login)."** A proxy won't help.
2. `playlist_max_connections`: **"Too many devices on this line."**
3. `decoder_failed`, `audio_failed`, `drm_failed`: **"Problem on the TV itself; the proxy won't
   help."**
4. The failure row says the proxy was off: **"Turn on the proxy."** This covers can't-reach-the-
   provider, refusal codes such as 513, an account check with no answer, and a web page where
   video should be.
5. Otherwise (proxy was on): **"Proxy is on and it still fails: provider or line problem."**

"Proxy on" is what the TV reported with the failure (`live_error.proxy`), not the switch on
kevbox.dev. After Kevin flips the switch, the next failure or play shows whether the TV picked
it up. The TV reads its proxy from kevbox.dev every 15 minutes and whenever the app comes back to
the screen (`KevBoxCoordinator`, `ManagedProxy`), so advice 4 adds: "The TV picks it up within 15
minutes, or at once if they reopen the app."

### Telegram

The check sends through Kevin's existing bot. `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID` go in
`/etc/kevbox-www/env` on persovps, copied from `~/.config/telegram.env`. They are never committed.

New problem:

> 🔴 KevBox Live: Nohad (client 23) can't watch since 20:45. The provider refused the channel
> list (code 513). Proxy is off → turn it on. <link to the customer window>

Problem cleared by a play note:

> 🟢 Nohad (client 23) is watching again, 20:58, proxy on.

Times are Asia/Beirut. The name and client number come from the workbook through
`member_playlist.client_number`, like the rest of kevbox.dev. A member with no client number
shows the email.

One message per customer, never one per TV: a customer with two failing TVs gets one message
that names both models.

When 3 or more customers open a problem on the same provider server (`provider_host`) within the
same 15 minutes, the check sends one message instead of one per customer: "4 customers can't
watch since 21:00, all on line.tellytv.pro. Most likely the provider is down; the proxy won't
help." Their "back" messages are combined the same way.

Between 00:00 and 08:00 Asia/Beirut, "can't watch" messages go out silently (Telegram's
`disable_notification`): they arrive, but the phone does not buzz. "Watching again" messages are
always silent.

A failed Telegram send leaves `alerted_at` empty, so the next run tries again. Telegram being down
never stops the incidents from being recorded or shown on kevbox.dev.

### kevbox.dev

- **Customer window, KevBox Live box**: one line beside the proxy switch, per TV when the customer
  has more than one. Green "Watching OK, last good play 20:58". Red "Can't watch since 20:45:
  turn on the proxy" (the advice). Grey "Old app (5.1.0.14): update to see watch status" for a
  TV below 5.1.0.15. Grey "No plays reported yet" for a new-enough TV that has not played. Grey
  "No plays for 7 days" as above.
- **Live errors page**: a "Can't watch now" list at the top with customer, client number, TV
  model, since when, the failure in words and the advice. A row opens the customer window. The
  list is empty when nobody is in trouble, and says so.

## Acceptance criteria

### TV

- **AC-1** A live channel that plays 30 seconds without an error, stop or channel change, in the
  preview box or full screen, makes the TV send a play note with device id, app version, TV-clock
  time and the proxy flag.
- **AC-2** A channel that plays 29 seconds and then fails, stops or changes sends no note.
- **AC-3** With no failure recorded in this process, a TV sends at most one note per hour.
- **AC-4** The first 30-second play after a failure recorded in this process sends a note at once,
  even within the hour.
- **AC-5** A failed send is retried on the next tick; only the newest note is kept.
- **AC-6** The note never carries a channel name, stream address, username or password.
- **AC-7** VOD, catch-up and multiview playback send no notes.
- **AC-8** No new user-facing strings. Fork rules hold: `// KevBox:` comments on changed upstream
  lines, the fork-only header on new files, `check-divergence.sh` passes.

### Database

- **AC-9** `0016_live_watch.sql` is idempotent, adds the three `live_device` columns and the
  `live_watch_incident` table, and grants `kevbox_admin` what the check needs.
- **AC-10** `record_live_play` updates only the caller's own row (`auth.uid()`), creates it when
  missing, and rejects a call with no signed-in user.
- **AC-11** Members can read only their own rows. Nobody writes the tables directly.

### Check and alerts

- **AC-12** A TV on 5.1.0.15+ with a channel-list or account failure, no later play note and 15
  minutes passed opens one incident and one Telegram message, and no second message on later
  runs. One `stream_lost` report alone opens nothing; a second report with no play between them
  opens an incident 15 minutes after the second.
- **AC-13** A play note later than the failure clears the incident and sends one "watching again"
  message with the proxy flag of that play.
- **AC-14** Non-counting codes (`guide_*`, `net_offline`, `behind_live_window`) never open an
  incident.
- **AC-15** TVs below 5.1.0.15 and failures older than 24 hours never open an incident.
- **AC-16** The advice follows the five rules in order. A unit test covers each rule, Nohad's 513
  with proxy off ("Turn on the proxy") and the same with proxy on.
- **AC-17** 3 or more customers opening incidents on the same `provider_host` within 15 minutes
  produce one combined message. A customer with two failing TVs gets one message naming both.
- **AC-17a** A "can't watch" message sent between 00:00 and 08:00 Asia/Beirut carries
  `disable_notification: true`; one sent at 08:00 or later does not. Every "watching again"
  message carries `disable_notification: true`.
- **AC-17c** A cancelled customer, or one whose `trex_expiry_date` is before today (Asia/Beirut),
  never gets a message; the customer window shows grey "Subscription ended <date>" or
  "Cancelled".
- **AC-17b** An incident with no play for 7 days shows grey on kevbox.dev and never sends another
  message.
- **AC-18** A failed Telegram send is retried on the next run. Missing Telegram settings log a
  warning and the check still records incidents.
- **AC-19** The check route requires `CRON_SECRET`, like the other cron routes. A systemd timer
  runs it every 5 minutes, with the same OnFailure alert.

### kevbox.dev

- **AC-20** The customer window's KevBox Live box shows the green, red or grey line per TV,
  including "Old app (<version>): update to see watch status" for a TV below 5.1.0.15.
- **AC-21** The Live errors page shows the "Can't watch now" list. Each row opens the customer
  window.

## Decisions

- Grill: ran 2026-10-15, two rounds, ended with nothing open. Round 1: one playback failure alone
  does not count (channel-list and account failures do); "can't watch" pings are silent
  00:00-08:00 Beirut; one message per customer; provider-down batching at 3 customers on one
  provider server; grey "Old app" line below 5.1.0.15; the proxy advice says when the TV picks it
  up; problems never re-ping and turn grey after 7 days. Round 2: cancelled and expired customers
  never ping; no dismiss button; no ignore list for Kevin's own or test accounts; "watching again"
  is always silent.

## Testing

- App: unit tests for the 30-second rule, the hourly limit, send-at-once after a failure, retry,
  and nothing sent for VOD. The Gradle lock wrapper runs the suite.
- kevbox: SQL tests for `record_live_play` against the test schema, unit tests for incident
  open/clear, the five advice rules, the 24-hour and version cut-offs, batching, and the Telegram
  retry. Route tests for the cron secret. Component tests for the status line and the list.
- End to end on the emulator against a local Supabase: fail a channel, wait, see the incident;
  play 30 seconds, see it clear.
